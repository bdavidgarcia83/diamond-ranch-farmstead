The Armenian Gampr: a guide for new owners

We breed Armenian Gamprs on our ranch in Hereford, Arizona, near Sierra Vista. These are the questions we hear most from people thinking about a Gampr for their place.

What is a Gampr?

The Gampr is the ancient livestock and family guardian dog of the Armenian Highlands and the southern Caucasus. For thousands of years shepherds there have kept them with their flocks to protect sheep and goats from wolves and other predators.

Gamprs are a landrace: a local type developed for a purpose and chosen for function rather than a standard appearance. That's why Gamprs can look quite different from one another while sharing the same ability. The Armenian Gampr Club of America defines a Gampr by its aboriginal ancestry and the absence of other breeds in its genetic history, not by looks.

In 2011 the International Kennel Union recognized the Gampr as the national dog of Armenia. In the United States, Gamprs are registered through the Armenian Gampr Club of America (AGCA). We're AGCA approved breeders with the club's Breeder of Merit designation.

How big do Gamprs get?

Gamprs are large, powerful and athletic, with strong bone and a body slightly longer than it is tall. Males are noticeably taller than females and have bigger heads. The Armenian breed standard calls for males from about 25 inches at the shoulder and females from about 23 inches, and adults commonly weigh around 110 to 130 pounds or more.

Their coat is thick, with longer guard hairs over a dense, soft undercoat. It can be long or short. The AGCA accepts any color except merle or blue. Expect heavy shedding twice a year, when the undercoat comes out in big patches.

What are they like?

Gamprs are independent, intelligent and confident. They were bred to make their own decisions out on the pasture, so they size up a situation before they respond and aren't commanded the way other dogs are. They bond deeply with their family and are known for being gentle with children. They have a strong urge to patrol, especially at dawn and dusk, and they notice anything out of place. Toward predators and strangers on their ground they're serious.

You can see that range in our own dogs. Dozer is patient enough to be trusted with chickens, ducks and newborn lambs and kids, and the barn cats love him. Riley runs the place and is great with newborn stock. Read their stories on Dozer's and Riley's pages.

How does a Gampr compare to other guardian dogs?

Gamprs do the same job as the Anatolian Shepherd, Great Pyrenees and other livestock guardian breeds: they live with the stock and keep predators away. The main differences:

  • Landrace, not a standardized breed. Gamprs come from working dogs in Armenia that were selected for function. Most familiar guardian breeds have closed stud books and written standards that shape their looks.
  • Not the same as its neighbors. Several breeds from the same part of the world are related to or descended from Gamprs, but the AGCA does not consider them Gamprs. That includes the Kangal, Anatolian Shepherd, Akbash, Caucasian Ovcharka and Central Asian Shepherd. The Caucasian Ovcharka, for example, is a heavier, standardized breed shaped by Soviet-era breeding.
  • Rare in the U.S. There are far fewer Gamprs than Anatolians or Pyrenees here, so choose a breeder carefully. The AGCA publishes a list of questions to ask a breeder.

Is a Gampr right for your place?

Gamprs do best as working guardians. They're a good fit if you have:

  • Livestock to protect, or acreage to patrol
  • Secure fencing
  • Room for a big dog that barks at night to warn predators off
  • Interest in a guardian rather than a house pet

They're usually not the right choice for a small lot in town or for someone who wants an obedience-trained companion.

Raising a Gampr puppy

A guardian learns its job by living with the animals it will protect. Our puppies are born and raised around our goats, sheep, chickens and cats, and they learn from their parents and from Nilla, our crossbred guardian who helps train the young dogs. When you bring one home, keep it with your stock, supervise it while it's young, and teach basic handling like walking on a leash.

Gamprs in the southeast Arizona desert

Our Gamprs live and work year-round on our high-desert ranch in Hereford, guarding Nigerian Dwarf goats, Dorper Katahdin cross sheep and Delaware chickens. The AGCA describes the Gampr's thick coat as excellent protection in all weather extremes. Like any guardian here, they still need shade and plenty of water in the summer heat.

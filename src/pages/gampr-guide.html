The Armenian Gampr: a guide for new owners

We breed Armenian Gamprs on our ranch in Hereford, Arizona, near Sierra Vista. These are the questions we hear most from people thinking about a Gampr for their place.

What is a Gampr?

The Gampr is the traditional livestock guardian dog of the Armenian Highlands. For thousands of years shepherds there have kept them with their flocks to protect sheep and goats from wolves and other predators. Gamprs are a landrace rather than a show breed: they were bred for the work, not for looks, so you'll see variety in coat and color.

In 2011 the International Kennel Union recognized the Gampr as the national dog of Armenia. In the United States, Gamprs are registered and tracked by the Armenian Gampr Club of America (AGCA), and we're AGCA approved breeders.

How big do Gamprs get?

Gamprs are large, athletic dogs. The Armenian breed standard calls for males from about 25 inches at the shoulder and females from about 23 inches, with adults commonly around 110 to 130 pounds or more. Their coat can be long or short, always with a soft undercoat, and any color is accepted.

What are they like?

Gamprs are independent, intelligent and strong-willed. They were bred to make their own decisions out on the pasture, so they think before they act and don't wait for commands. With their own family and livestock they're calm, gentle and loyal. Toward predators and strangers on their ground they're serious.

You can see that range in our own dogs. Dozer is patient enough to be trusted with chickens, ducks and newborn lambs and kids, and the barn cats love him. Riley runs the place and is great with newborn stock. Read their stories on Dozer's and Riley's pages.

How does a Gampr compare to other guardian dogs?

Gamprs do the same job as the Anatolian Shepherd, Great Pyrenees and other livestock guardian breeds: they live with the stock and keep predators away. The main differences:

  • Landrace, not a standardized breed. Gamprs come from working dogs in Armenia that were selected for function. Most familiar guardian breeds have closed stud books and written standards that shape their looks.
  • Built to move. Gamprs are generally lighter and more athletic than the heavy-built Caucasian Ovcharka, a breed developed in the Soviet era partly from the same regional landrace dogs.
  • Rare in the U.S. There are far fewer Gamprs than Anatolians or Pyrenees here, so it's worth finding a breeder who imports or keeps good records and health tests.

Is a Gampr right for your place?

Gamprs do best as working guardians. They're a good fit if you have:

  • Livestock to protect, or acreage to patrol
  • Secure fencing
  • Room for a big dog that barks at night to warn predators off
  • Interest in a guardian rather than a house pet

They're usually not the right choice for a small lot in town or for someone who wants an obedience-trained companion.

Raising a Gampr puppy

A guardian learns its job by living with the animals it will protect. Our puppies are born and raised around our goats, sheep, chickens and cats, and they learn from their parents and from Nilla, our crossbred guardian who helps train the young dogs. When you bring one home, keep it with your stock, supervise it while it's young, and teach basic handling like walking on a leash.

Gamprs in the southeast Arizona desert

Our Gamprs live and work year-round on our high-desert ranch in Hereford, guarding Nigerian Dwarf goats, Dorper Katahdin cross sheep and Delaware chickens. Gamprs evolved in the mountains of Armenia with a thick double coat, so like any guardian here they need shade and plenty of water in the summer heat.
